# Review: Does an employment gap on your resume hurt? (`employment-gaps.md`)

Verdict **revise**: 1 high, 10 medium, 12 low findings, all open. Every number the draft takes
from its 17 sources matches the source. The problems are what it leaves out or reads too
kindly. The published summary of the one US reason-line test is open and says the gain was
significant, while the draft says three times that nobody can tell if it was chance. The
length table mixes an unadjusted figure (1-6 months) with adjusted ones (13+ months) and has
no 7-12 month row, exactly where the "six months or more" advice sits. The review's own overall
effect (about -7%, borderline) is missing, so a reader sees only the big long-gap numbers. One
US field test of past gaps and stop-gap jobs (Nunley 2017) is missing, which makes one "What we
don't know" line wrong. The stop-gap section reports men only. "A layoff did best" and "say so
in one line" rest on a test with no silent arm. "Employers check" and the health-question law
are stated wider than their sources.

### Searched for newer or contrary work (2026-10-04)

- **Nunley, Pugh, Romero, Seals 2017**, ILR Review 70(3), 642-669 (abstract opened on a
  reference page; listed in dhert-2026 Table 3). US resume audit, recent college graduates.
  "no statistical evidence linking unemployment spells of different durations to employment
  opportunities"; underemployed graduates got callbacks 30% lower. The review's table adds:
  former unemployment had no effect. **Missing from the draft** - finding M4.
- **Norlander and others 2020**, Basic and Applied Social Psychology (abstract opened on the
  university page; listed in dhert-2026 Table 3). Lab studies: stressing an outside,
  uncontrollable cause "alleviates discrimination". Field study: discrimination "occurs nearly
  instantaneously" after job loss (1 month out of work). Contrary to "short gaps cost
  nothing"; supports a "not my fault" reason only in the lab. Finding M10.
- **ResumeGo 2019** (vendor page opened): 36,510 applications, reason given 6.8% vs none 4.3%
  ("58.1% higher"). No test of chance, no peer review; the company sells resume writing.
  The program's notes already reject it. Readers will meet this number elsewhere - finding L9.
- **Ghayad 2013/2014**, "The Jobless Trap": working paper, callbacks fall in the first six
  months. Still not found in a journal. Leaving it out is right; no finding.
- **Newer field tests (2025-2026)**: none published that we found. Two registered trials seen
  in search results only, not opened: Italy (long-term unemployment, 2025) and one on exam
  preparation gaps (to March 2026). "Every field test here ran in 2020 or earlier" holds.
- **Law**: EEOC guidance page answers today, not withdrawn. No change found.

## Findings

- **H1 (high) - The reason-line test's published summary is open and says the gain was
  significant.** Lines 16, 63, 68, 111, 131. The draft uses the 2018 conference abstract and
  says "no test of chance in the open summary" and "does not say whether the difference could
  be chance". The published abstract (JEBO 185, 865-882; open at
  `https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jeborg/v185y2021icp865-882.html`) says the illness-gap
  callback is "significantly higher than those whose employment gap is unexplained" and that a
  credible explanation "can substantially reduce its scarring effect". Fix: cite significance
  from the published abstract; drop both chance sentences; keep "the three rates come from the
  authors' 2018 summary and may differ in the printed paper"; registry venue gets volume +
  pages + the RePEc URL. Also say the illness was cancer, the gap was seven months or more
  (trial registry), and the resume listed a cancer recovery support group besides the letter.
  Same stale wording in `what-makes-a-good-resume.md` lines 41, 101, 163 - revise bead fixes
  both. status: open
- **M1 (medium) - Length table mixes two kinds of estimate.** Lines 14, 21, 25, 35-37. The
  1-6 month figure (+8.23%) is unadjusted; the 13-18 and 19-36 figures are covariate-adjusted
  (all p. 1382). The authors' adjusted figure for 1-6 months, outliers removed, is +16.73%
  [8.91, 25.10], and their abstract says short spells "positively affect" chances. The draft's
  reading is the cautious one and can stay. Fix: one sentence ("After the authors adjusted for
  study differences, short spells showed a small plus") and a table note naming which figures
  are adjusted. status: open
- **M2 (medium) - No 7-12 month row.** Lines 25, 33-37. The advice line sits at six months;
  the table jumps from 6 to 13. The journal's main text gives no figure. Fix: open supplement
  Table A3 and add the row, or add "7-12 months: no figure in the article's main text". Do not
  publish the 2024 preprint's figure as the journal's. status: open
- **M3 (medium) - The review's overall result is missing.** Line 25. Overall effect -7.31%
  [-14.40, 0.36] unadjusted, -7.89% [-14.67, -0.56] adjusted; with outliers removed the
  interval spans zero (p. 1380). Each extra month: -1.71% (p. 1383). Without these the reader
  sees only the largest numbers. Fix: one sentence with the overall figure and "borderline".
  status: open
- **M4 (medium) - A US field test of past gaps and stop-gap jobs is missing.** Lines 31, 95,
  126. Nunley and others 2017 (ILR Review 70(3), 642-669, doi 10.1177/0019793916654686): US
  college graduates, no link between unemployment spells and callbacks, former unemployment no
  effect (per dhert-2026 Table 3), underemployment -30%. So "The clearest test of past gaps is
  Swedish" is wrong, "three large US tests" is short by one, and the stop-gap section misses
  its closest study. Only the abstract was opened here. Fix: open the paper, register it, add
  a table row and a stop-gap sentence, reword line 126 to what is still unknown (past gaps for
  older or non-graduate US workers). status: open
- **M5 (medium) - Stop-gap section reports men only.** Line 95. Pedulla's women: full-time
  10.4%, below-skill job 5.2% (significant), a year out of work not significantly lower. Temp
  agency work was not clearly penalized for either. Fix: add "For women in the same test, a
  below-level job also cut callbacks, but a year out of work showed no clear cost. Temp agency
  work showed no clear cost for men or women." status: open
