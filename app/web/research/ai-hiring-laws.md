**Short answer**

- Legal in the US: no federal law bans AI resume screening, as of October 2026. Old discrimination laws still apply (Law, Court record) [@mobley-2026-order].
- Notice now: New York City, Illinois. Later: Colorado 2027, Connecticut 2027, EU 2027 (Law) [@nyc-ll144; @il-hb3773; @co-sb26-189; @ct-pa26-15; @eu-omnibus-2026].
- Explanation + human review: Colorado only, from January 2027 (Law) [@co-sb26-189].
- Enforcement thin: 18 of 391 New York City employers had posted an audit (Real records) [@wright-2024].

This page describes laws as of October 2026. It is general information, not legal advice. Which law covers a job depends on where it is and who the employer is.

## Is it legal for employers to use AI to screen resumes?

Yes, in the US as of October 2026. No federal law bans employers from using AI to read or rank resumes. A few places add rules on top, mostly about telling you.

The old discrimination laws still cover what the software does. In June 2026, a federal court in California let a lawsuit over AI screening go on. The claims going on include race, disability and age under federal law, plus California state-law claims. The court decided only which claims may continue, not whether they are true [@mobley-2026-order, p. 1-2, 11].

For you, a law against discrimination applies whether a person or a program turns you down.

## Which laws make employers tell you they use AI?

### New York City

As of October 2026, New York City Local Law 144 covers automated tools that help decide who gets hired. An employer using one needs a bias audit done within the past year. A summary of the audit results must be public. Candidates who live in the city must get notice 10 business days before the tool is used. The city has enforced the law since July 5, 2023 [@nyc-ll144].

The city's rule says how notice works, as of October 2026. Notice can go on the careers page, in the job posting, or by mail or email. It must explain how to ask for another selection process or an accommodation. The rule does not make the employer offer another process. You can also ask in writing what data the tool uses and where it comes from. The employer must answer within 30 days, unless that information is already on its website [@nyc-aedt-rule, section 5-304]

### Illinois

As of October 2026, Illinois treats some AI use in hiring as a civil rights violation. The law covers AI that has the effect of discriminating against a protected group. Using ZIP codes as a stand-in for a protected group also counts. Failing to tell applicants that AI is used is a violation too. The law, Public Act 103-0804, took effect January 1, 2026 [@il-hb3773].

We found no final Illinois rule on what the notice must say, as of October 2026.

### Colorado

As of October 2026, Colorado's SB26-189 takes effect January 1, 2027. The law covers automated systems used in decisions such as hiring. Employers must give clear notice when you deal with such a system. After a decision goes against you, they must explain it in plain words within 30 days. You may correct personal data that is factually wrong. You may ask for "meaningful human review and reconsideration" of the decision. The state Attorney General enforces the law. The law gives applicants no new right to sue. The law, signed May 14, 2026, replaced Colorado's 2024 AI law [@co-sb26-189].

A separate Colorado law covers age, as of October 2026. A first job application may not ask your age, birth date or school dates. Employers must tell you that you may black out those dates on transcripts and certificates. The state labor department has enforced the law since July 1, 2024 [@co-sb23-058].

### Connecticut

As of October 2026, Connecticut Public Act 26-15 adds notice duties from October 1, 2027. Employers must say in plain words when you deal with an automated hiring tool, unless that is obvious. Before a decision, they must give written notice of what the tool is and what it decides. It names the product, the kinds of personal data used and their sources. The Attorney General enforces these parts, and applicants get no new right to sue. A separate section, in force since October 1, 2026, says using such a tool is no defense to a discrimination complaint [@ct-pa26-15, sections 7-13].

Some summaries online say Connecticut gives you a right to an explanation or to fix your data. The act's text, sections 7 to 13, does not grant either one [@ct-pa26-15].

### The European Union

As of October 2026, the EU AI Act lists hiring AI as high-risk. That covers AI that filters job applications and evaluates candidates. Employers must tell people when such a system helps make decisions about them. A person affected by such a decision may ask for a clear explanation of the AI's role [@eu-ai-act, Annex III point 4, Articles 26, 86]. A 2026 change moved the start of these hiring rules to December 2, 2027 [@eu-omnibus-2026].

For you, notice is the most common right. An explanation or a human second look is still rare.

## Which laws limit how AI may judge you?

As of October 2026, California's rules took effect October 1, 2025. An automated system may break California law if it harms applicants because of a protected trait. Employers must keep records, including the system's data, for four years. A test or puzzle that draws out disability information may count as an unlawful medical question [@ca-crc-ads-2025].

As of October 2026, New Jersey's civil rights rules took effect in December 2025. The rules name automated online application tools and face-analysis software in hiring. Either may cause unlawful unequal effects under New Jersey's anti-discrimination law [@nj-dcr-2025].

As of October 2026, the federal disability law, the ADA, applies before any job offer. Employers may not ask disability questions before an offer. You may ask for a reasonable accommodation in the hiring process itself [@eeoc-ada-preemployment].

## What is the federal government doing about AI hiring laws?

An April 2025 executive order set a policy to end disparate-impact liability "in all contexts." Disparate impact means a neutral-looking practice that harms one group more. The order told agencies to deprioritize enforcing it [@eo-14281].

A December 2025 executive order set up a federal task force to challenge state AI laws. The order names Colorado's law as an example [@eo-14365]. In April 2026, the Justice Department asked to join a company's lawsuit against Colorado's 2024 AI law. The department argues that law breaks the Constitution's equal protection rule [@doj-xai-2026]. Colorado replaced the 2024 law with SB26-189 in May 2026 [@co-sb26-189]. We found no court ruling on the challenge as of October 2026.

The executive orders tell federal agencies what to focus on. They did not stop the June 2026 court order letting federal discrimination claims over AI screening go on [@mobley-2026-order].

## Are AI hiring laws enforced?

In a 2024 study, 155 student investigators checked 391 employers' websites. Only 18 employers had posted a bias audit report. Only 13 had posted a notice to candidates. The authors warn that the law gives employers "substantial discretion" over whether their tool is covered. So a missing audit does not prove an employer broke the law [@wright-2024, p. 1, 8].

A December 2025 state audit looked at the city's enforcement from July 2023 to June 2025. The city reviewed 32 companies and found one issue. State auditors looked at the same companies and found at least 17 possible problems. The city received two complaints in two years. The audit called the city's complaint process "ineffective" [@nys-comptroller-2025].

For you, a law on paper does not mean every employer follows it.

## What about the Workday lawsuit?

Mobley v. Workday is a US lawsuit still in progress, as of October 2026. The plaintiffs allege Workday's screening tools discriminated by race, age and disability [@mobley-2026-order, p. 1]. In May 2025 the court let the age claim go forward for a wider group of applicants, at a preliminary stage [@clearinghouse-mobley]. In September 2026 the plaintiffs asked to add four groups of applicants, with a hearing set for March 2027. Workday denies the claims, and nothing has been proven [@lawyer-monthly-2026].

## What helps

- Read the job posting and careers page for an AI notice. New York City allows notice there [@nyc-aedt-rule].
- In New York City, ask in writing what data a tool uses. The employer has 30 days to answer [@nyc-aedt-rule].
- If an AI test or game does not work for you because of a disability, ask for an accommodation [@eeoc-ada-preemployment].
- In Colorado from January 2027, ask for the explanation and a human review after a rejection [@co-sb26-189].
- Keep a copy of each notice and what you sent. The rights above run on dates: 10 business days, 30 days.
- Remember bias in a screener is the employer's problem, not a flaw in you.
