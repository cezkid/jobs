# What makes a good resume

What CEZ Job Finder holds every resume to, and why. Each point says how strong the evidence is: a
**big survey**, a **small study**, or **convention** (what career centres and recruiters agree
on, without a study behind it). Each section links to the full evidence and sources.

When rules pull against each other, this order decides:
**true > shows real results > fits this job > easy to read.**

## 1. Every word is true

- **Employer, job title and dates match what the employer's records say.** Over three-quarters
  of employers found a mismatch on a candidate's resume last year, work history most often.
  *Big survey (HireRight 2025).*
- **Every number is one you can explain in an interview.** Anything on the page gets asked about.
  CEZ Job Finder never adds a number, tool or qualification you did not give it. *Convention, and
  the reason for the rest of this list.*
- **No grades you can't prove** - "world-class", "cutting-edge". The fact that earned the praise
  says more. *Convention (career centres).*

In depth: [accuracy rules and sources](../app/docs/resume/bullets.md#tier-1---accuracy)

## 2. Lines show results, not duties

- **Say what changed, or how big it was** - how many people, screens, patients, dollars; how
  much faster or cheaper. A number lets a reader picture the work. Not every line needs one.
  *Convention, stated by every career centre checked.*
- **Every part of a line adds something.** Cut what is true of anyone in the role.
  *Convention.*
- **Show how you worked with people** - what you led, started, taught or did with other teams -
  when it really happened. *Convention.*

Missing a number? Say "help me add numbers to my resume" - the chat asks, you answer, and only
your answers go in. In depth: [what counts as evidence](../app/docs/resume/bullets.md#tier-2---substance)

## 3. It fits the job

- **Use the job posting's own words for things you really have.** Recruiters search for the
  posting's terms. A term you don't have is a gap to know about, never a word to add.
  *Convention, backed by recruiting-software documentation.*
- **Most relevant first.** The first line under each job is the one always read. *Convention.*
- **About the last 10-15 years.** Older jobs can be shortened; never leave a hole in the middle.
  *Convention.*
- **Explain long breaks.** A one-line reason answers the question a silent gap raises. Long
  unexplained gaps get screened out at about half of employers. *Big study (Harvard Business
  School / Accenture 2021).*

In depth: [fitting the job](../app/docs/resume/bullets.md#tier-3---relevance)

## 4. It reads cleanly - to people and to software

- **One idea per line; one line or two full ones.** A line with a few words alone on the last
  row wastes space. *Convention; the page is measured, not guessed.*
- **US spelling, no typos, no repeated openings, no filler** ("successfully", "I", "my").
  Screening tools count one spelling error against the whole page. *Convention.*
- **A plain page software reads in order:** one column, black text, no tables, pictures or
  headers, every word readable back whole. CEZ Job Finder checks this on every resume it makes, so
  you never have to. *Recruiting-software documentation.*
- **One or two pages.** *Convention.*
- **Leave off** photo, birth date, marital status and street address. US employers don't ask for
  them and they invite bias. City and state is enough. *Convention.*

## Common advice we don't follow

- **"Put a number on every line."** Pushes people to make numbers up. We ask for the real one.
- **"Add these keywords."** Only words for things you really have.
- **"75% of resumes are rejected by software."** The figure traces to a defunct vendor with no
  method; most recruiters say their systems don't auto-reject.
