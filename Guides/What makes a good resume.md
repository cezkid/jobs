# What makes a good resume

The rules every resume here follows. Each says how strong its evidence is:
**big study** (thousands of real applications) · **small study** · **survey** · **vendor survey** (by a
company that sells the service) · **lab study** (not real hiring) · **convention** (what career
centres + recruiters agree on, no study) · **our measurement**. Some rules rest on hiring software's
own help pages - said so.

Full version, with every source: https://jobs.enrriquez.com/research/what-makes-a-good-resume/

Rules clash? This order wins: **true > real results > fits the job > easy to read.**

## 1. Every word true

- **Employer, title, dates match the employer's records.** Over 3 in 4 employers found a mismatch
  last year, work history most. *Vendor survey (HireRight 2025, a background-check company).*
- **Every number one you can explain.** Interviews ask about anything on the page. Nothing
  added you didn't give. *Convention.*
- **No self-praise** ("world-class"). The fact behind it says more. *Convention.*

More: [accuracy](../app/docs/resume/bullets.md#tier-1---accuracy)

## 2. Results, not duties

- **Say what changed or how big** - people, dollars, how much faster. Not every line needs a
  number. *Convention.*
- **Cut what's true of anyone in the role.** *Convention.*
- **Show working with people** - led, started, taught - when it happened. *Convention.*

Missing numbers? Say "help me add numbers to my resume" - only your answers go in.
More: [evidence](../app/docs/resume/bullets.md#tier-2---substance)

## 3. Fits the job

- **Posting's own words, for what you really have.** Recruiters search them. Missing = a gap
  to know, never a word to add. *Convention + hiring software's help pages.*
- **Most relevant first.** First line under each job always gets read. *Convention.*
- **Last 10-15 years.** Shorten older jobs; no hole in the middle. *Convention.*
- **Breaks over 6 months: add a one-line reason.** Over a year: that line, plus any recent work,
  study or volunteering you really did. In one survey, about half the executives whose
  software sorts applicants said it filters out breaks over 6 months. Longer breaks cost more. In
  one US study a reason won back about half the lost replies from employers.
  *Survey (2020) + big study across 7 countries; the reason's effect is one small study.*

More: [fitting the job](../app/docs/resume/bullets.md#tier-3---relevance)

## 4. Reads cleanly - people + software

- **One idea per line; one full line or two.** A few words alone on a row waste space.
  *Convention; our measurement.*
- **US spelling, no typos, no filler** ("successfully", "I"). *Convention.*
- **Plain page:** one column, black text, no tables or pictures. Checked on every resume.
  *Hiring software's help pages.*
- **1-2 pages.** *Convention.*
- **Leave off** photo, birth date, marital status, street address - invite bias. City + state
  is enough. *Convention.*

## Advice we don't follow

- **"A number on every line."** Pushes made-up numbers. We ask for the real one.
- **"Add these keywords."** Only for what you have.
- **"75% of resumes rejected by software."** Traces to a defunct vendor, no method. In one
  small vendor survey, 23 of 25 recruiters said their systems don't reject for formatting,
  content or design. Where software rejects alone, it's mostly yes/no questions.
