# For students

Internships, co-ops, a part-time job alongside classes, your first full-time job after
graduating: tell the chat what you're after, in your own words. "I'm a student looking for a
summer internship" is enough.

## Finding jobs

- **Internships and co-ops:** Job Finder reads each job's title as well as the job search's own
  label - that label calls many summer internships "full time".
- **First job after graduating:** jobs asking 3 or more years of experience sort lower on your
  list. Never hidden: you decide.
- **Who can apply:** many internships say it, like "graduating between December 2027 and June
  2028". Job Finder reads that line and compares it with your graduation date. Outside it, the job
  sorts lower and says why.
- **Pay:** internships and part-time jobs usually pay by the hour, so give your lowest pay that way
  ("$17 an hour").
- **Your school's own job board** (Handshake, a career fair, a posting a professor sent): paste the
  posting into the chat and say "make my resume for this job".
- **Timing:** big employers often fill summer internships months ahead, many starting in the fall.
  Your career centre knows your field's calendar.

## Your resume

- While you're a student, **Education goes first**, with "Expected May 2027". It keeps saying
  Expected until you tell the chat you finished.
- **Classes, projects, clubs and part-time jobs all count.** A class project goes under Projects.
  A club where you held a role prints like a job: "Treasurer | Robotics Club".
- **GPA:** your call whether it shows. In a 2026 survey of 183 US employers, 42% said they screen
  by GPA (73% did in 2019), and many applications ask for it anyway. When it shows, it's exactly
  your transcript's figure - never rounded up or converted from another scale.
- **Relevant coursework:** a few courses the jobs you want ask about, named as your school names
  them.
- **Time in school between summer jobs isn't a break.** Tell the chat when you started school and
  it stops counting as one.
- **Clubs and groups:** how each shows is your call - its full name, a general description you
  write ("Treasurer, 40-member student group"), or left off. Job Finder never renames one.
  Worried about bias? Ask any time, or read
  [Unfair hiring - what's known, what helps](Unfair%20hiring%20-%20what's%20known,%20what%20helps.md).
- **Email:** many schools close student email some time after you graduate. A personal address you'll
  keep is safer on a resume (common advice, not a study).
- **Your career centre** reviews resumes and runs practice interviews, usually free.

## Applying

- Your expected graduation date, GPA and "Are you currently enrolled?" are filled from your
  resume. You see each one before you press Submit.
- None of these is saved for the next form: they change.
- Practice interviews work for you too: stories can come from a class project, a club, a team or a
  part-time job. Say "practise my interview for job 3".

## International students (F-1)

General information, not legal advice. Your school's international student office has the final
word on your case.

- **Off campus, every job needs approval first:** CPT while you study, OPT around or after
  graduation - unpaid internships too. On-campus jobs don't, up to 20 hours a week while classes
  are in session.
- **Form questions:**
  - "Legally authorized to work in the US?" - many school offices say Yes if you have, or will apply
    for, CPT or OPT.
  - "Authorized to work in the US without restriction?" - the offices we checked (Carnegie Mellon,
    UC Irvine) say No. Job Finder asks you on every form, never answers it for you.
  - "Will you need sponsorship now or in the future?" - Yes for most F-1 students: a work visa
    after OPT ends.
- **"No visa sponsorship" jobs** sort lower for you. CPT and OPT need no sponsorship from the
  employer, so read the posting before you rule one out.
- **Plan ahead:** 12 months or more of full-time CPT ends your OPT; STEM OPT needs an employer
  enrolled in E-Verify. Ask your office before you accept.

## Unpaid internships

Federal wage law, as of October 2026: a company may leave an intern unpaid only when the internship
mainly benefits the intern. Courts weigh 7 factors - ties to your school or credit, fitting the
school calendar, not replacing paid staff, no promised job - and none decides alone. Non-profits
and government offices may take unpaid volunteers. Your state may be stricter. General
information, not legal advice. For comparison: bachelor's-level interns averaged $23.35 an hour in
summer 2025 (employer survey).

## Job scams

From the Federal Trade Commission's advice:

- An honest employer never asks you to pay to get a job.
- A check to deposit, then money to send back, is a scam - the check bounces and you owe the bank.
- Big pay for little work, or an offer with no application or interview: be wary.
- Lost money? Call your bank or payment app right away, then report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.

## AI plans for students

- **GitHub Copilot Student** (free for verified students) has used only its automatic model since
  June 2026. Job Finder hasn't tested it; Copilot's free plan on that automatic model failed 3 of 3
  tailored resumes. Copilot Pro ($10 a month) lets you pick a stronger AI and keeps the rest of the
  Student Pack - switch off AI training on it:
  [Keep your chats out of AI training](Keep%20your%20chats%20out%20of%20AI%20training.md).
- **A school AI account** (ChatGPT Edu, Claude for Education) belongs to your school: not used for
  AI training by default, your school decides what its admins can see, and you may lose it after
  you graduate. It runs Job Finder only if your school turned on Codex (ChatGPT) or Claude Code -
  your school's IT help desk can say.
